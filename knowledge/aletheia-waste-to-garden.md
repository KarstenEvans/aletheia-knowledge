## Evidence labels

- STRONG: well-established horticultural or composting principle supported by strong current guidance.
- GOOD: reliable practical guidance, though details vary by system/cultivar/context.
- PLAUSIBLE: biologically workable, but outcomes are variable or evidence is less formal.
- CAUTION: workable only with important safety, biosecurity or context limitations.
- NOVELTY: mainly decorative/experimental rather than an efficient growing method.

## WTG-COMP-001 | What composting actually is
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: compost basics
KEYWORDS: decomposition humus microbes organic matter
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: What composting actually is gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Home composting is controlled decomposition of organic material. Microbes and small invertebrates convert mixed plant and food residues into a darker, more stable soil-improving material.

### How
Mix varied organic inputs, keep the mass moist rather than waterlogged, and allow air to reach it. Time does most of the work.

### Technical
Carbon supplies microbial energy; nitrogen supports proteins and growth. Water enables biochemical reactions and oxygen supports aerobic decomposition.

### Pros
Returns nutrients and organic matter to soil, reduces waste, and can reduce reliance on bought soil improvers.

### Caveats
Finished garden compost is not the same thing as sterile seed compost or a precisely formulated potting medium.

### Sources
- https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/expert-advice/garden-management/composting
- https://compost.css.cornell.edu/chemistry.html

## WTG-COMP-002 | Greens and browns
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: green brown balance
KEYWORDS: greens browns nitrogen carbon cardboard grass kitchen waste
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Greens and browns gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
A practical household rule is to combine moist, nitrogen-rich 'greens' with drier, carbon-rich 'browns'. Garden Organic suggests roughly equal volumes as an easy starting point.

### How
For each bucket of kitchen scraps or grass, add about a bucket of torn cardboard, dry leaves, straw or similar brown material, then adjust by smell and texture.

### Technical
The visual labels 'green' and 'brown' describe function more than colour. Coffee grounds are physically brown but behave more like a nitrogen-rich green.

### Pros
Simple enough to use without weighing ingredients.

### Caveats
Equal volumes do not equal a chemically exact carbon:nitrogen ratio, so treat this as a practical control rule rather than laboratory precision.

## WTG-COMP-003 | Carbon:nitrogen ratio
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: C:N ratio
KEYWORDS: carbon nitrogen 30:1 C:N microbes
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Carbon:nitrogen ratio gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
For active composting, a starting carbon:nitrogen ratio around 30:1 by weight is a widely used technical target.

### How
Most home gardeners should not calculate every ingredient. Use the ratio to understand problems: too much readily available nitrogen can smell of ammonia; too much resistant carbon can make decomposition slow.

### Technical
Cornell notes that finished compost commonly trends toward roughly 10–15:1 as carbon is lost as carbon dioxide during decomposition.

### Pros
Useful for diagnosing mixtures and for advanced compost calculators.

### Caveats
The ideal varies with how biologically available each material is. A nominal C:N value does not tell you everything about decomposition speed.

### Sources
- https://compost.css.cornell.edu/chemistry.html
- https://compost.css.cornell.edu/calc/cn_ratio.html

## WTG-COMP-004 | Moisture control
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: compost moisture
KEYWORDS: wet dry squeeze moisture waterlogging
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Moisture control gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Compost organisms need water, but saturated compost loses air and can turn anaerobic. Very dry material decomposes slowly.

### How
Aim for evenly moist material. If it drips or slumps into slime, add dry browns and improve structure. If dusty and inactive, add water gradually while mixing.

### Technical
Water and air occupy the same pore spaces. As water fills those spaces, oxygen diffusion falls sharply.

### Pros
Fixing moisture often fixes smell and speed at the same time.

### Caveats
There is no single household moisture percentage you need to measure; texture and behaviour are usually more useful.

- https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/expert-advice/garden-management/composting/how-to-make-compost

## WTG-COMP-005 | Oxygen and aeration
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: aerobic compost
KEYWORDS: oxygen aeration turning anaerobic structure
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Oxygen and aeration gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Most garden composting works best aerobically. Air reaches a heap through pore spaces, coarse material and occasional mixing.

### How
If the heap smells sulphurous or rancid, add dry structured browns such as torn cardboard and mix compacted material. Turning speeds decomposition but is not mandatory for slow compost.

### Technical
Turning redistributes oxygen and moves cooler outer material toward the more active centre.

### Pros
Faster decomposition and fewer anaerobic odours.

### Caveats
Over-aerating a small heap can dry it out, especially if the bin has very open sides.

## WTG-COMP-006 | Particle size
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: chop shred size
KEYWORDS: shredding chopping surface area woody waste
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Particle size gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Smaller pieces generally decompose faster because microbes gain more surface area and the heap mixes more evenly.

### How
Cut fibrous stalks and hedge material into shorter pieces; shred woody prunings if practical. Do not pulverise everything into a dense mat.

### Technical
Very fine wet material can compact and lose air, so particle-size reduction should be balanced with structure.

### Pros
Especially useful for stems, cardboard and woody material.

### Caveats
Shredders cost energy and money and are not necessary for soft kitchen waste.

## WTG-COMP-007 | Temperature and hot heaps
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: hot compost
KEYWORDS: temperature thermophilic 45 60 70 weed seeds
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Temperature and hot heaps gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Large, well-balanced heaps can heat strongly because microbial respiration releases heat. Hot composting is faster and can suppress many weed seeds and pathogens.

### How
Build a sufficiently large, mixed batch, monitor moisture, and turn as the temperature falls if you want an actively managed hot heap.

### Technical
Mesophilic organisms dominate at moderate temperatures; thermophilic microbes become important as temperatures rise. Domestic heaps often fail to sustain industrial-style temperatures because they are too small or inconsistently fed.

### Pros
Fast processing and better seed/pathogen reduction when genuinely hot throughout.

### Caveats
Do not assume a warm heap has sanitised everything. Cool edges and short heat exposure can leave weed seeds or pathogens viable.

## WTG-COMP-008 | Cold composting
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: slow compost
KEYWORDS: cold heap low effort passive compost
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Cold composting gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Cold composting accepts material gradually and relies on time rather than sustained high temperatures.

### How
Keep adding a sensible mix of greens and browns. Turn when convenient, not because the system will fail without it.

### Technical
Lower temperatures mean slower decomposition and poorer destruction of weed seeds and some disease material.

### Pros
Low effort, suitable for ordinary household waste streams.

### Caveats
May take many months to a year or more and requires more care over what weeds or diseased plants are added.

## WTG-COMP-009 | High-fibre household compost
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: high fibre method
KEYWORDS: cardboard kitchen waste worms no turn household
EVIDENCE: GOOD
DEEP_QUERY: High-fibre household compost gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
A useful small-garden strategy is to pair kitchen/garden greens with plenty of crumpled paper and card, creating a moist but structured heap that can work without repeated turning.

### How
Add torn or crumpled card regularly with soft green waste. Avoid deep mats of grass. Let composting worms colonise naturally where conditions suit them.

### Technical
The method trades maximum heat for reliable structure, moisture buffering and worm activity.

### Pros
Works with the continuous trickle of waste produced by a normal household.

### Caveats
Do not copy old advice blindly: modern packaging may include plastic films, coatings or synthetic fibres that should not enter home compost.

## WTG-COMP-010 | Grass clippings
SECTION: COMPOST
KEY: grass compost
KEYWORDS: lawn mowings nitrogen slime mulch
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Grass clippings gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university
BOOK: Compost: The Natural Way to Make Food for Your Garden — Ken Thompson (DK, 2007), project editor Annelise Evans
BOOK_URL: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/9780756613419/Compost-Natural-Way-Food-Garden-0756613418/plp

### Summary
Fresh grass is nitrogen-rich and water-rich. In thick layers it compacts rapidly and can become slimy and anaerobic.

### How
Mix grass thoroughly with torn cardboard, dry leaves or other browns. Alternatively leave fine clippings on the lawn or use thin layers as mulch where appropriate.

### Technical
The problem is physical as much as chemical: wet blades collapse, excluding air.

### Pros
A valuable nitrogen source and plentiful summer material.

### Caveats
Avoid thick fresh mats against plant stems; they can heat, smell and become water-repellent as they dry.

### Summary
Plain uncoated cardboard is a useful carbon-rich compost ingredient and an excellent counterbalance to kitchen scraps and grass.

### How
Remove plastic tape, labels and non-paper windows. Tear or wet large sheets before mixing. Egg boxes and cardboard tubes are especially convenient.

### Technical
Corrugations and crumpled pieces add temporary air spaces as well as carbon.

### Pros
Cheap, widely available and useful for correcting a wet heap.

### Caveats
Avoid heavily plasticised, foil-lined, waxed or uncertain coated packaging. Packaging technology changes faster than compost folklore.

### Summary
Uncooked fruit and vegetable scraps are standard 'green' compost ingredients.

### How
Cut large dense pieces smaller and mix them below or among browns rather than dumping a wet bucket in one layer.

## WTG-REUS-015 | Do not use crocks as a drainage layer
SECTION: REUSE
KEY: crocks myth
KEYWORDS: broken pots drainage gravel layer perched water table
EVIDENCE: STRONG
DEEP_QUERY: Do not use crocks as a drainage layer gardening evidence RHS Garden Organic university

### Summary
Putting a thick layer of stones or broken pots beneath potting mix does not improve drainage in the way commonly claimed and can reduce the useful root-zone depth.

### How
Use a container with open drainage holes and a suitable free-draining potting mix. A single shard may cover a very large hole without sealing it, but do not build a rubble layer.

### Technical
Water movement changes at boundaries between different pore sizes, so a coarse layer can leave the finer compost above wetter rather than drier.

### Pros
More root volume and simpler pot preparation.

### Caveats
This is a useful example of why Waste to Garden includes an evidence checker rather than repeating every old gardening hack.

### Summary
A quiet natural nesting site is often the simplest hedgehog shelter. Gaps under sheds, leaf piles, log piles, shrubs and compost heaps can all be used. Where extra shelter is useful, a sturdy reused storage box or large planter can be turned into a simple hedgehog house.

### How
Choose a quiet shady spot. Use a sturdy ventilated container that cannot collapse, turn it upside down if appropriate, and provide an entrance around 13cm x 13cm. ### Summary
Untreated offcuts of solid wood or hollow bamboo can provide nesting cavities for solitary bees such as red mason bees and some leaf-cutting bees.

### How
For a wood block, use untreated wood at least 10cm deep and drill smooth-ended holes about 2-10mm wide as deeply as practical without drilling through the back. ### Caveats
Rough splintered holes can damage wings. Avoid chemically treated wood. Nesting tubes and blocks need maintenance and eventual replacement rather than being left indefinitely.

## WTG-REUS-019 | Amphibian hibernaculum from logs, bricks and rubble

EVIDENCE: GOOD

### Summary
Old logs, clean bricks, stone and rubble can be arranged into a frost-protected hibernaculum where newts, frogs, toads and some reptiles can shelter through winter.

### How
Choose a well-drained spot, preferably shaded or partly shaded. Lay logs, old bricks or clean rubble so that many small cavities remain. Loosely fill some gaps with woodchip, then cover much of the structure with soil, turf, moss or vegetation while leaving access gaps.

The cavities provide stable temperature and humidity. Good drainage matters because a refuge that floods or becomes waterlogged can become dangerous rather than protective.

### Summary
One of the highest-value forms of garden reuse is simply not removing every fallen leaf, twig and piece of dead wood. A quiet log pile, leaf pile, dead hedge or untidy corner creates shelter and food chains for many species.

### How
Choose a low-traffic corner, stack logs and prunings securely, allow some leaves to accumulate, and avoid frequent disturbance. Keep loose netting and hazardous rubbish out.

### Technical
Decaying wood and leaf litter support fungi and invertebrates. Those invertebrates become prey for hedgehogs, amphibians, birds and other garden wildlife, so one pile can serve several trophic levels.

### Pros
Free, low-effort and often more ecologically useful than decorative manufactured wildlife products.

### Caveats
Check piles carefully before moving or burning them. An occupied hedgehog nest or protected-species refuge should not be disturbed.
