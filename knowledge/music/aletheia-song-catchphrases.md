# Aletheia Song Catchphrases

**Status:** Working cross-artist knowledge base  
**Shelf:** [Aletheia Music](index.md)  
**Last research pass:** 2026-09-24

A song catchphrase is a small piece of language that survives outside its song. It may be a title, refrain, command, question or short lyric fragment. Aletheia tracks **what makes it memorable**, not just where it came from.

# Detailed seed cards

## SC-001 — Nothing ever lasts forever

**Song:** Everybody Wants to Rule the World  
**Artist:** Tears for Fears  
**Writers:** Roland Orzabal, Ian Stanley, Chris Hughes  
**Year:** 1985  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE

**Vibe:** impermanence · melancholy · reassurance · mortality · change · freedom

**Why it sticks:** four ordinary words; "ever" echoes inside "forever"; it is absolute but leaves the object unstated. Love, power, pain, youth or success can all be inserted by the listener.

**Emotional hinge:** good things end, which hurts; bad things end, which comforts.

**Walkabout:** "this too shall pass"; impermanence; memento mori; changing political power; ageing; relationships; George Harrison's *All Things Must Pass*; Echo & the Bunnymen's *Nothing Lasts Forever*.

**Artist:** [Tears for Fears knowledge](aletheia-tears-for-fears.md)

## SC-002 — Everybody wants to rule the world

**Artist:** Tears for Fears  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** power · ambition · control · freedom · politics

A sweeping statement about human behaviour. "Everybody" deliberately overgeneralises; "rule the world" is huge, visual and simple. It works in politics, offices, families, games and jokes.

## SC-003 — Mad world

**Artist:** Tears for Fears  
**Writer:** Roland Orzabal  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** alienation · absurdity · sadness · overwhelm

Two words scale a private feeling up to an entire world.

### Audiovisual case study — Kiwi!

Dony Permedi's 2006 student animation *Kiwi!* originally used music by Tim Cassell. A later fan edit paired the same images with the Gary Jules / Michael Andrews recording of *Mad World*. The changed soundtrack can make the same visual sequence feel much more tragic.

**Aletheia principle:** image + music + words can change the interpretation of one another. Preserve this as a case study in audiovisual semantic interaction.

## SC-004 — Hey Jude

**Artist:** The Beatles  
**Writer:** Paul McCartney; credited Lennon–McCartney  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** reassurance · affection · resilience · communal release

The song evolved from "Hey Jules", McCartney's idea for Julian Lennon during John and Cynthia Lennon's separation. A private act of reassurance became a public singalong.

**Walkabout:** names as hooks; tiny wording changes; divorce and friendship; private language becoming cultural language.

**Sources:** [The Beatles](https://www.thebeatles.com/hey-jude) · [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/the-beatles-hey-jude/)

## SC-005 — Let it be

**Artist:** The Beatles  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** acceptance · calm · surrender · reassurance

Three words can mean stop interfering, accept what cannot be changed, allow time to work, or leave something alone. Its power comes partly from the tension between wise acceptance and passive resignation.

## SC-006 — All you need is love

**Artist:** The Beatles  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** idealism · universality · simplicity · peace

A complete philosophy shaped like an advertising slogan. The interesting question is not only why it is memorable, but where its simplification is illuminating and where real life resists it.

**Chart evidence:** UK No. 1.  
**Source:** [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/the-beatles-all-you-need-is-love/)

## SC-007 — Life on Mars?

**Artist:** David Bowie  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** wonder · estrangement · escape · absurdity

Three words and a question mark open an entire imagined world. It is both cosmic and colloquial: a giant question asked in everyday language.

**Chart evidence:** UK No. 3.  
**Source:** [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/david-bowie-life-on-mars/)

## SC-008 — Let's dance

**Artist:** David Bowie  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE  
**Vibe:** invitation · motion · intimacy · immediacy

Two common words form an instruction that the music itself can fulfil. The listener does not merely understand the phrase; the listener can obey it.

**Chart evidence:** UK No. 1.  
**Source:** [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/david-bowie-lets-dance/)

## SC-011 — No woman, no cry

**Artist:** Bob Marley & The Wailers  
**Class:** CORE CATCHPHRASE via 1981 UK reissue  
**Vibe:** consolation · endurance · memory · community

Its syntax is memorable partly because many listeners initially parse the phrase differently from its Jamaican usage. That ambiguity helped it travel across cultures even while the song's emotional centre is consolation.

**Chart evidence:** 1981 release reached UK No. 8.  
**Source:** [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/bob-marley-the-wailers-no-woman-no-cry-1981/)

## Descriptive fingerprint for future app

Do not rate artistic quality. Store descriptive traits that can power a future Song Vibe generator:

- brevity
- repetition
- ordinary vocabulary
- unusual syntax
- rhyme / internal echo
- question / command / statement
- ambiguity
- emotional polarity
- universality
- visual imagery
- physical/action language
- out-of-song usefulness
- cultural afterlife
- audiovisual reframing

The database supplies dots. The future Aletheia Song Vibe experience joins them into new pictures.
