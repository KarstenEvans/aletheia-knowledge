## Story and factual reality

In the fictional case, visible AI interfaces disappear while software systems continue adapting routes, rankings and other decisions. The real-world question is **where a decision is made, what influences it, and what oversight is possible**, not whether all automated software qualifies as "AI".

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) describes governance, mapping, measurement and management of risks over the AI lifecycle, including human oversight and documenting system purpose and performance. It does not prescribe Aletheia's nine fictional questions.

The [UK Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/how-do-we-ensure-individual-rights-in-our-ai-systems/) explains that automation bias, limited interpretability and the lack of meaningful human involvement can create risks around AI-assisted decisions. Whether UK GDPR Article 22 applies depends on the actual processing and consequences; the story is not legal advice.

## The nine Aletheia decision questions

1. What decision is being made?
2. What are we assuming?
3. What evidence do we have?
4. Can we challenge the strongest assumption?
5. If it goes wrong, how could it happen?
6. What information is missing?
7. Which options can we remove?
8. What uncertainty remains?
9. Do we have enough to stop?

These are project-specific learning prompts. They are not a legal checklist or direct quotation from NIST/ICO.

## Five practical safeguards

- Map hidden automation and document where the model or rule operates.
- Distinguish visible UI from background processing.
- Keep meaningful evidence and the responsible owner identifiable.
- Offer human review or escalation appropriate to the consequences.
- Set a stop condition and clearly label unresolved uncertainty.
