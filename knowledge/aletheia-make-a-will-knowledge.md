# Aletheia Make a Will Knowledge

Useful first: four independent family-planning tasks. This collection **explains** UK rules and options and links to the [portable Make a Will system](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/aletheia-app/blob/main/aletheia-make-a-will/aletheia-make-a-will.md). It does not draft a signable will or create a family trust.

## WIL-001 | Ordinary wills
A will identifies beneficiaries, executors, alternative beneficiaries and any specific gifts. Parents each need their **own** will. A home owned as beneficial joint tenants normally passes to the other joint tenant outside the deceased's will; a tenant-in-common share can pass through the estate. An ordinary will in England and Wales requires correct written signing and witnesses; the source document must be found after death. Complicated families, foreign property, existing trusts and vulnerable dependants need legal advice. Sources: [GOV.UK Making a will](https://www.gov.uk/make-will) · [Witness requirements](https://www.gov.uk/make-will/make-sure-your-will-is-legal) · [Joint ownership](https://www.gov.uk/joint-property-ownership).

## WIL-002 | Family handover
Keep a separate, updateable inventory of the **names and locations of providers and records**: banks, ISAs, pensions, insurance, house title, utilities, bills, digital-account recovery procedures and the location of the original signed will. Do not put passwords, PINs, recovery codes or complete bank account numbers into a public document or chatbot. The deceased's sole bank accounts are normally frozen; a qualifying joint account usually continues for the survivor, subject to conditions. [Tell Us Once](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/organisations-you-need-to-contact-and-tell-us-once) does not notify private banks and utilities. Source: [MoneyHelper bereavement finance](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/family-and-care/death-and-bereavement/dealing-with-finances-and-insurance-after-your-partner-dies.html).

## WIL-003 | DIY probate
Start with death registration, finding the original will and executor, protecting property, recording assets/debts, inheritance-tax reporting, checking whether a grant is needed, then paying debts and distributing safely. Some estates do not need probate. The executor can often apply without a solicitor. Relevant fees and probate requirements must be rechecked at the time of death. Sources: [GOV.UK after death](https://www.gov.uk/when-someone-dies) · [GOV.UK probate](https://www.gov.uk/applying-for-probate).

## WIL-004 | Build the family legacy
Ordinary families can consider leaving property individually, contributing to a discretionary trust or organising investments through a family investment company, but **tax and operating costs** can outweigh benefits at ordinary asset levels. A family discussion and written goals cost nothing. If pooling money, record who contributed what, who benefits, who governs, loans/grants, annual reporting and a fair exit process. Trusts can have creation, ten-year and exit tax charges; companies have accounting and shareholder tax. For most new English private trusts, a 125-year perpetuity period commonly applies. Sources: [Trusts and IHT](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/trusts-and-inheritance-tax) · [Trust income tax](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/trusts-and-income-tax) · [Trust registration](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/registering-a-trust).

## WIL-005 | Home, gifts and taxable estate
The basic inheritance-tax nil-rate band is £325,000 and qualifying homes may attract an additional residence nil-rate band of up to £175,000 when inherited by direct descendants, subject to all conditions. Spouse/civil-partner exemptions and transfers of unused bands are relevant in some cases. Ordinary outright gifts to individuals can become exempt after seven years, **but gifting a home and continuing to live in it rent-free typically causes a gift-with-reservation** so the value may still be in the estate. There is no seven-year rule automatically protecting assets from care-fee assessments. Source: [IHT overview](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax) · [Gifts](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/gifts) · [Passing on a home](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/passing-on-home).

## WIL-006 | Pensions, SIPPs and nominations
A defined contribution pension may support nominated beneficiaries, but defined benefit survivor pensions and annuities operate differently. Most unused pension benefits enter UK IHT consideration for deaths on/after 6 April 2027, subject to exclusions and updated implementation rules. This is not a guarantee that pension funds bypass an estate or reach any particular beneficiary. Source: [HMRC pensions technical note](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-on-pensions-technical-note/technical-note-inheritance-tax-on-pensions).

## WIL-007 | First-home support, ISAs and family gifts
An eligible adult can open a Lifetime ISA before turning 40, deposit up to £4,000 each tax year until 50 and obtain a 25% government bonus subject to scheme and first-home conditions. A parent over 60 may **gift** their adult child money; the parent does not open a LISA in the child's name. Junior ISAs are owned by the child, not the family. The 2026 Budget proposes a replacement First Time Buyer ISA from April 2029 with no upper age restriction; it is **not available yet**. A repayable deposit loan must be disclosed honestly to the mortgage lender. Sources: [LISA](https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa) · [Junior ISA](https://www.gov.uk/junior-individual-savings-accounts) · [Budget 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-document/budget-2026-html).

## WIL-008 | Free help, paid kits and useful books
Free official sources: GOV.UK, MoneyHelper, Citizens Advice. Subject to eligibility, Will Aid and Free Wills Month sometimes provide free or donation-supported solicitor will-writing. Paid printed forms can help some simple families, but are not personalised legal advice; examples include Lawpack standard will form, Ryman Lawpack kits. Optional wider reading: *The Psychology of Money* (ISBN 9780857197689) for behaviour, not inheritance law; current probate books require edition checks. [Aletheia Make a Will resources](https://karstenevans.github.io/aletheia-app/aletheia-make-a-will/aletheia-make-a-will-rsc.htm) has links and commercial transparency.

## WIL-009 | Protect the family and avoid aggressive sales
A solicitor is not mandatory as professional executor. Verify a legal service firm's regulatory registration, scope, extra estate administration and storage fees, and future executor appointment terms. Avoid firms promising a trust will make the family home invisible to IHT or care assessments. Sources: [SRA register](https://www.sra.org.uk/consumers/register/) · [MoneyHelper will writing services](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/family-and-care/death-and-bereavement/will-writing-services-pros-and-cons.html).
