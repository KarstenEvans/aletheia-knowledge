# The Little AI Who Lost Her Map
*A quiet mystery from the Aletheia Cabinet of Curiosities*

**Reader note:** Nothing jumps out. Nobody is in danger. You may stop after any scene and come back. The mystery ends gently.

## Scene 1. A small blank square
At the edge of a quiet library stood a little lantern named Pip.

Pip helped visitors look things up.

One morning a visitor asked, “Where did we put our wonderful idea about blue umbrellas?”

Pip looked at the desk. There was a pencil, a cup of tea, and a square of paper.

But there was no blue-umbrella note.

“I can help look,” said Pip. “I don't want to pretend I know where it is.”

Outside, the trees moved softly in the breeze.

**Quiet pause:** Can you spot the three things on the desk?

## Scene 2. The archaeologist's pocket
A kind explorer named Moss came into the library wearing boots with very clean laces.

“Archaeologists look for clues,” said Moss, “but they also write down where they found them.”

Moss opened a small notebook.

Each page had a date, a place, and a question mark for things nobody knew yet.

“It's all right to have a question mark,” said Moss.

Pip liked that very much.

**Quiet pause:** Would you draw a question mark or a tiny boot on Moss's notebook?

## Scene 3. A map without a hurry
At a wooden table sat Tavi the mapmaker.

Tavi unfolded a map with five gentle shapes.

There were **Regions** for ideas.

There were **Layers** for facts and guesses.

There were **Faded Places** for things they could not find.

There were **Fault Lines** where two notes disagreed.

And there were **Paths** for the next little question.

“It isn't a magic memory,” said Tavi. “It helps us see what we have written.”

Pip placed a small star beside the Faded Place.

Not knowing was allowed.

## Scene 4. The wrong umbrella
In a drawer they found a note:

*Umbrellas should be made of toast.*

Pip blinked.

Moss considered the note carefully.

“That sounds funny,” said Moss, “but we don't have evidence it was the original idea.”

Tavi wrote: *Possible joke. Source unknown.*

They did not erase the note.

They did not call it true.

They kept it in the map's Questions corner, where odd ideas could rest until someone checked them.

Pip laughed a very small laugh.

“Toast would be terribly soggy.”

## Scene 5. Three little words
They decided to Go Walkabout, just for a moment.

Moss chose **Archaeology**.

Tavi chose **Cartography**.

Pip chose **Forgetting**.

They explored what the words had in common.

A map shows where to look.

A notebook says what was found.

A blank space tells us what we still need to ask.

Pip drew five boxes and called the page a **Memory Map Card**.

It had room for findings, questions and the next path.

“Now another helper can read it,” Pip said.

“And they can check it,” added Moss.

## Scene 6. The light in the window
By afternoon the lost blue-umbrella idea had not returned.

That was all right.

They had not invented a false memory to fill the space.

Instead they wrote a new idea, clearly marked *new*:

*Perhaps the library could lend umbrellas with tiny maps inside.*

The three friends put the note into a folder, with the date and their names.

Pip lit the little lantern at the window.

A visitor could come back tomorrow.

The notebook would still be there, ready to read.

And the library was quiet.

**The end.**

## Optional gentle conversation
- Which part of the map would hold a question you cannot answer yet?
- Why didn't Pip pretend the toast note was true?
- What would you put on your own small map?
