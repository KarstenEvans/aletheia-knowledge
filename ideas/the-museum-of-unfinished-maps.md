# The Museum of Unfinished Maps
*A new Walkabout from archaeology, forgetting and cartography*

**Reader note:** Six short scenes. You may stop whenever you like. There is no hurry, no peril and no correct answer you have to guess.

## Scene 1 — A Museum with an Empty Label

Every Thursday, eleven-year-old Neri visited the little Museum of Everyday Things.

Most exhibits had ordinary names: **One Blue Teacup**, **An Old Train Ticket**, **The World's Smallest Umbrella Repair**.

But today a glass case held a single paper square. Its label read:

**ITEM 47: WE KNOW THIS MATTERED. WE DON'T KNOW WHY.**

Neri liked mysteries, especially the ones nobody pretended to have solved.

She asked the museum keeper, Aunt Sol, if they might investigate.

“We can,” said Sol. “First we write down what we know.”

On a fresh card, Neri wrote: *A square of paper. Found in the town archive. Purpose unknown.*

It was a small beginning. Small beginnings counted.

*Quiet pause: What would you write on the label?*

## Scene 2 — The Floor Beneath the Floor

Under the museum was a room filled with carefully numbered trays.

Sol called it the sorting room.

One tray held drawings of streets from a hundred years ago. Another held newspaper clippings. A third held objects that had been found together.

“Archaeologists learn from layers,” Sol explained. “But a layer is a clue, not a complete story.”

Neri noticed that the paper square had been discovered beside a sketch of the old railway platform.

She wrote: *Found near railway drawing. Connection uncertain.*

“Do we know they belong together?” she asked.

“Not yet,” said Sol.

Neri drew a little dotted line between the two entries.

Dotted lines, she decided, were rather useful.

## Scene 3 — The Cartographer's Table

The museum's oldest table was covered with maps.

One map showed streets. Another showed where people had planted gardens. A third was blank except for a few carefully written question marks.

That third map belonged to Professor Quill, a retired cartographer who volunteered on Thursdays.

“A good map can show what we don't know,” Quill said.

Together they made a new map with five kinds of marks.

Circles showed **places and topics**.

Stacked paper shapes showed **evidence and guesses**.

Open squares showed **missing information**.

Zigzags showed **contradictions**.

Tiny footprints showed **where to look next**.

Neri called it a **Memory Map Card**.

“Does it remember things for us?” she asked.

“It holds what we recorded,” said Quill. “We still have to check.”

*Quiet pause: Which mark would you use for an unanswered question?*

## Scene 4 — The Two Timetables

In a drawer, Neri found two copies of an old train timetable.

One said the town's last morning train left at eight.

The other said nine.

“That means one is wrong!” she said.

“Possibly,” replied Sol. “Or perhaps they are from different years.”

Neri checked the dates. The pages came from different summers.

A mystery that had looked like a mistake became a lesson in context.

She drew a zigzag between the timetables, then added a note: *Difference explained by dates.*

“Shall I erase the zigzag?” she asked.

“No need,” said Quill. “Let the map show what we checked and corrected.”

The record grew clearer without pretending the confusion had never happened.

## Scene 5 — A Place for the Missing

By the end of the afternoon, they still did not know what the paper square had once been used for.

Neri had an idea.

She placed an empty square on the map and wrote: **UNKNOWN, FOR NOW**.

There was room beneath it for a source, a date and a future discovery.

At the bottom she added a note for the next Thursday visitor:

*Please start here. We found a clue near the railway drawing, but we have not proved the connection.*

Quill smiled.

“A useful map doesn't need every road drawn in.”

That evening, the paper square remained unsolved.

But the investigation was no longer lost.

## Scene 6 — The Visitor Who Came Next

A week later, Neri was away visiting her cousin.

A new museum volunteer named Ari arrived and found the Memory Map Card.

He followed the circles to the town archive, read the dotted-line note and checked the dates on the timetables.

Then he discovered a photograph: a row of coloured paper squares pinned to a noticeboard for a children's art exhibition.

The square in the case looked similar.

Ari wanted to write **MYSTERY SOLVED**.

Instead he wrote: *Promising photograph; match not yet confirmed.*

He added the photo's archive number and left space for Neri to continue.

When Neri returned, she could see exactly what Ari had found and what he hadn't.

There was still a question to answer.

And now there were two people who knew where to begin.

**The end.**

**Closing line:** *You don't have to remember everything to find something wonderful.*
